Lecture 04: Leadership styles

This is fictional workshop material, not official UWA course content.

## Situational leadership

Situational leadership says there is no single best way to lead. The right style depends on a follower's readiness for a given task: their competence and their confidence. A new team member on an unfamiliar task usually needs clear direction. A capable, confident team member usually needs autonomy and support instead of instructions.

## Transformational versus transactional leadership

Transactional leadership works through a clear exchange: meet the expectation, get the reward; miss it, face the consequence. It is dependable for routine, well-defined work. Transformational leadership works by connecting people to a larger purpose, raising their expectations of themselves and each other. It tends to matter most when a team needs to change how it works, not just do more of the same.

## Choosing an approach

A leader rarely uses one style all the time. Situational leadership argues for matching the style to the follower and the task. Transformational leadership is not "better" in every case; a transactional approach can be exactly right when a task is routine and the team already knows what good looks like.
