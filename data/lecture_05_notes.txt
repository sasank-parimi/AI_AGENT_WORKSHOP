Lecture 05: Organisational culture and team dynamics

This is fictional workshop material, not official UWA course content.

## Organisational culture

Organisational culture has visible and hidden layers. Artifacts are what you can see and hear straight away: dress code, office layout, how meetings run. Espoused values are what the organisation says it stands for, often written down in a mission statement. Basic assumptions sit underneath both, rarely said out loud, and are the hardest to shift because people are not always aware they hold them.

## Team dynamics

Tuckman's stages describe how a team tends to develop over time: forming (polite, cautious), storming (disagreement surfaces), norming (the team settles on how it will work together), and performing (the team works well without needing to renegotiate the basics). Teams can slip backward, especially when membership changes or a task gets harder than expected.

## Why this matters together

Culture shapes how comfortable a team feels moving through storming. A culture that treats disagreement as useful lets a team reach norming faster than a culture that treats disagreement as a problem to avoid.
